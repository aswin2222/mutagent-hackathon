Quick start

Quick start

1 Open your project

A new folder or an existing repo. The system installs into it.

$ mkdir my-agents && cd my-agents
  # or: cd your-existing-repo

2 Install the system

Install the CLI, sign in, then add the agents and skills into .claude/ and .codex/.

$ npm install -g @mutagent/cli   # or pnpm / bun
$ mutagent login
$ mutagent install helix

3 Boot

Start your coding agent, then summon the orchestrator.

$ claude   # or codex
❯ *mutagent   # or /mutagent-helix

How it works

How it works: one orchestrator, many subagents

Helix guides you through your agent development process. Jump in anywhere; nothing moves on without you.

① SPEC ② BUILD ③ EVALUATE ④ DIAGNOSE ⑤ OPTIMIZE

Drive the lifecycle: say a stage, or just what you want

① SPEC*specdescribe your agent, get a validated spec file
② BUILD*buildturn the spec into a working agent
③ EVALUATE*evaluatecheck how good it is: pass or fail per behavior
④ DIAGNOSE*diagnosefind out why it failed: ranked fixes
⑤ OPTIMIZEgatedapply the fix, then re-check until it passes

First time? Start with *onboard

Add your provider keys, pick your workspace, and choose your models. Then jump into any stage.

Two ways to drive it: type a *command, or just say what you want. Helix routes it to the right stage and waits for you.

Spec

① Spec: turn an idea into a working spec

❯ *spec "design a Support Triage agent that reads Zendesk threads and drafts replies"

A short guided interview captures what your agent is, then writes a spec file you can build from. You answer with quick pick-lists.

❯ *spec
  What agent do you want to build?
❯ A Mastra agent that triages our support
  inbox and drafts replies for approval.
  ⟨pick-lists⟩ what kind? · how should it
   talk to your tools?
❯ ⟨taps options⟩
  wrote your spec done · next: *build
   (I won't move on without you)

Features

  • Decide the right architecture first, in a 30-minute conversation, not after weeks of rework.
  • Explicit input and output contracts, so multi-agent handoffs can't fail silently.
  • A written, framework-agnostic spec the whole team can build from, not tribal knowledge.

Build

② Build: your spec becomes a real, working agent

❯ *build "build the Support Triage agent from its spec on Mastra"

What the reviewer checks

A second pass confirms the build is faithful to your spec. Every capability you asked for is actually there, before you move on. No silent gaps.

Features

  • Generates against the spec, tests to green, with a reviewer that checks nothing is missing.
  • Every capability in the spec ships implemented and tested, never stubbed or silently skipped.
  • Model and dependency drift becomes a test failure, not a silent prod incident.

Evaluate

③ Evaluate: four ways to know if your agent is good

❯ *evaluate "evaluate the Support Triage agent on last week's 150 Zendesk threads"

Four ways to evaluate

  • FINDSuggests what's worth testing, mined from your runs.
  • SCOREPass or fail per behavior. Built-in judge, or a test-suite in your own CI.
  • CREATEWrite the checks yourself, guided by your spec.
  • DATATurn a few examples into a realistic set of test cases.

Features

  • Evals mined from your real traces, plus synthetic cases generated from your spec when you need coverage you do not have yet.
  • A judge you can trust: same input, same verdict every run.
  • Your first eval set on day one, no blank-page cold start.
  • A stable pass/fail gate for CI, plus a continuous score for direction.
Producesa calibrated eval suite and evaluation reports from the judge

Diagnose

④ Diagnose: when an eval fails, find out why

❯ *diagnose "diagnose the Support Triage agent, refund threads keep failing the policy check"

Features

  • Root-cause across thousands of traces, not one at a time on gut feeling.
  • Tells you why, not just what, with cited evidence: the file, line, and message that caused it.
  • Catches runaway loops and token waste before the bill arrives.
  • Groups repeat failures into one root cause, so you fix the cause and not twenty symptoms.

Optimize

⑤ Optimize: apply the fix, then re-check

❯ *optimize "optimize the Support Triage agent until the refund criterion passes"

The EDD loop is the lifecycle's tight inner cycle: build, evaluate, diagnose, optimize, back to build, running until the eval gate passes.

Loops until it passes, gated by you

Hands the recommended fix to an AI engineer that edits your agent, then re-runs evaluate. It loops: fix, re-build, re-evaluate, until the agent passes. Nothing changes without your go-ahead.

Features

  • Verify the fix on your real traces before you ship, not after customers complain.
  • Every fix becomes a permanent regression test, so it can't silently come back.
  • Ships the way your target expects: a pull request for code, a platform API call for a hosted agent, or a direct update to the agent definition. Nothing changes without your go-ahead.
